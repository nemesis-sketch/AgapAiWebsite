About AgapAi

Why we built it

Picture Aling Teresa. Her teenage son has a severe laceration with arterial bleeding from broken sheet metal during a typhoon cleanup. Paramedics are 45 minutes or more away. Bystanders panic and put coffee grounds on the wound. The mobile signal is intermittent 2G.

Moments like this are why AgapAi exists.

  • Little to no first-aid training
  • Delayed emergency response
  • Harmful folk remedies
  • Apps that need strong 4G/5G
  • English-only medical jargon

Mission

To give zero-knowledge bystanders instant, offline-capable, camera-guided first-aid instructions in native Philippine languages, using materials they already have at home.

Vision

Smart vision, instant action, lives saved: a digital first responder that helps people act correctly in the minutes before professional help arrives.

AgapAi is built by Technology Innovators of the Philippines, a founding team of a CEO, a CTO and a CMO.

Products and services

AgapAi is a mobile app with three core capabilities.

Visual first-aid triage

Camera scanning powered by lightweight on-device vision models to classify wounds, burns and obstructions.

Step-by-step hands-free guidance

Multi-dialect audio pacing in Tagalog, Cebuano and Taglish, with a voice-guided CPR metronome at 100–120 BPM and bleeding control.

Offline-first improvised resources

Operates without active internet through edge processing, and identifies everyday household items such as malong, clean cloth or bamboo as safe substitutes.

First-aid coverage

In scope
  • External hemorrhage and wound dressing
  • Burns and motorcycle scalds (pasa sa tambutso)
  • Choking and airway clearing (Heimlich guidance)
  • Hands-only CPR audio pacing
  • Bone immobilization and improvised splints
Out of scope
  • Internal disease diagnosis (cardiac arrest pathology, stroke, chronic illness)
  • Drug dosing or prescription advice
  • Invasive procedures

Maneuvers are external and non-invasive, aligned with DOH and Philippine Red Cross standards.

Important: AgapAi provides first-aid guidance and does not replace professional medical assessment. In an emergency, call emergency services.

The guided flow

From the moment you open the AgapAI prototype, the guided process walks you through four stages: scanning the scene, analyzing the evidence and optional audio capture, following the six severe-bleeding stabilization steps, and handing over telemetry to emergency responders.
